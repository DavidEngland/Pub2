Section 2: Answer Key

  1. Bridge between MOST and K-theory: The bridge rests on equating their respective representations of turbulent fluxes. Specifically, it involves rearranging the flux-gradient relations of MOST to define eddy diffusivities (Km,KhK_m, K_hKm​,Kh​) and then equating them to mixing-length formulations that utilize stability correction functions (fm,fhf_m, f_hfm​,fh​).
  2. Computational Advantage: RiRiRi-based formulations can be expressed in closed form, eliminating the need for time-consuming iterative techniques required to solve for ζ\zetaζ. This allows for more computationally efficient numerical models and facilitates analytical dynamical analysis, such as the application of Bendixson’s criterion.
  3. Critical Richardson Number (RicRi_cRic​): RicRi_cRic​ represents the limiting value of the Richardson number (corresponding to ζ→∞\zeta \to \inftyζ→∞) where turbulent mixing shuts down continuously. It defines a finite upper limit for sustained turbulence in stable conditions, typically valued between 1/51/51/5 and 1/41/41/4.
  4. Prandtl Number (PrPrPr): PrPrPr is defined as the ratio of the exchange coefficients for momentum and heat (Km/KhK_m / K_hKm​/Kh​), or the ratio of their respective similarity functions (ϕh/ϕm\phi_h / \phi_mϕh​/ϕm​). It expresses the relative efficiency of the mixing of momentum versus the mixing of heat in the atmosphere.
  5. Quadratic Stability Functions: Quadratic forms provide a continuous derivative at the critical Richardson number, whereas linear forms often exhibit a slope discontinuity. This additional smoothness reduces numerical noise in space and time and produces more stable solutions in first-order closure models.
  6. Hysteresis and Predictability: Hysteresis refers to the existence of multi-valued equilibrium solutions (indicated by "S-shaped" bifurcation diagrams) where the system state depends on its past history. This behavior reduces SBL predictability, as small parameter shifts can cause the system to jump abruptly between cold, calm states and warm, windy states.
  7. Taylor Expansion/Series Reversion: When ζ(Rig)\zeta(Ri_g)ζ(Rig​) cannot be inverted in closed form, a Taylor expansion around near-neutral conditions (ζ→0\zeta \to 0ζ→0) yields a forward series for Rig(ζ)Ri_g(\zeta)Rig​(ζ).   8. Model Deterioration: Models often deteriorate because traditional local closures utilize stability functions with relatively sharp cut-offs at theoretical critical Richardson numbers. This leads to "decoupled" solutions that fail to account for non-local effects or intermittent turbulence, resulting in surface temperatures that are colder than observed.
  9. Surface Roughness (z0z_0z0​): In finite-difference models, the RiRiRi formulation for the lowest layer is transformed logarithmically to include explicit dependence on z0z_0z0​. Section 4: Glossary of Key Terms

Term Definition
Bifurcation Analysis A method of nonlinear dynamics used to explore how the steady-state solutions of a system (such as the SBL) change as external parameters (e.g., geostrophic wind) vary.
Closed-Form Solution An explicit mathematical expression evaluated in a finite number of operations, as opposed to iterative numerical root-finding.
Eddy Diffusivity (KKK) Coefficients (KmK_mKm​ for momentum, KhK_hKh​ for heat) used in first-order closures to relate turbulent fluxes to mean vertical gradients.
Flux-Profile Relationships Empirical non-dimensional functions (ϕm,ϕh\phi_m, \phi_hϕm​,ϕh​) describing surface-layer vertical gradients of wind and potential temperature as functions of stability.
Friction Velocity (u∗u_*u∗​) A surface-layer velocity scale defined by the kinematic momentum flux, u∗=(τ/ρ)1/2u_* = (\tau/\rho)^{1/2}u∗​=(τ/ρ)1/2.
Gradient Richardson Number (RigRi_gRig​) A dimensionless ratio measuring the relative importance of buoyant destruction/production of turbulence to mechanical shear production.
Hysteresis A property of dynamic systems where the current equilibrium state depends on historical trajectory, leading to multi-valued solution branches.
Monin–Obukhov Similarity Theory (MOST) A theoretical framework asserting that surface-layer non-dimensionalized profiles depend uniquely on the stability parameter ζ=z/L\zeta = z/Lζ=z/L.
Monin–Obukhov Length (LLL) A characteristic length scale representing the height where buoyant suppression/production of turbulence equals shear production.
